# Same as Ever: A Guide to What Never Changes

**Author:** Morgan Housel  
**Status:** Listened

## Core Thesis

Jeff Bezos once said that the most important question in business is not *"What's going to change in the next ten years?"*, but **"What's *not* going to change in the next ten years?"** While technology, markets, and headlines shift constantly, human behaviour (how people react to risk, greed, fear, envy, stress, and incentives) remains stubbornly constant. Studying what never changes is far more durable and useful than trying to forecast what happens next.

## Parts & Chapter Notes

1. **[Part 1: Risk and Expectations](part-1-risk-and-expectations.md)** (Ch 1–6)
    - **Ch 1: Hanging by a Thread:** Tiny accidents steering history, the Lake Tahoe avalanche, George Washington's wind shift, and FDR's missed assassin.
    - **Ch 2: Risk Is What You Don't See:** Harry Houdini's fatal sucker punch, blind-spot catastrophes, and preparing instead of predicting.
    - **Ch 3: Expectations and Reality:** The happiness equation (reality minus expectations), John D. Rockefeller vs. modern comfort, and the envy trap.
    - **Ch 4: Wild Minds:** Genius and madness sharing the same wiring, high-variance thinkers, and why you cannot cherry-pick someone's traits.
    - **Ch 5: Wild Numbers:** Our craving for false certainty in a probabilistic world, outlier-driven power laws, and why precision beats accuracy in sales pitches.
    - **Ch 6: Best Story Wins:** Narrative beating raw data, Stephen Hawking's equation rule, *Sapiens*, and stocks as numbers multiplied by stories.
2. **[Part 2: Human Nature and Incentives](part-2-human-nature-and-incentives.md)** (Ch 7–12)
    - **Ch 7: Does Not Compute:** Why other people's "irrational" choices make sense to them, hidden emotions, and why logic alone rarely persuades.
    - **Ch 8: Calm Plants the Seeds of Crazy:** Hyman Minsky's financial instability hypothesis, complacency during boom times, and why stability creates risk.
    - **Ch 9: Too Much, Too Soon, Too Fast:** Natural limits of scale, forcing growth too quickly, and patience versus impatience.
    - **Ch 10: When the Magic Happens:** Acute stress as the engine of breakthroughs, wartime innovation, and Great Depression productivity leaps.
    - **Ch 11: Overnight Tragedies and Long-Term Miracles:** Why bad news is instant while good news compounds invisibly, and Buffett's 20-year reputation rule.
    - **Ch 12: Tiny and Magnificent:** Small inputs compounding into magic or catastrophe, 3.8 billion years of evolution, and sustainable returns over peak returns.
3. **[Part 3: Progress and Compounding](part-3-progress-and-compounding.md)** (Ch 13–18)
    - **Ch 13: Elation and Despair:** Why pessimism sounds smarter than optimism, and saving like a pessimist while investing like an optimist.
    - **Ch 14: Casualties of Perfection:** Why over-optimisation breeds fragility, evolutionary slack, and Amos Tversky's case for being a little underemployed.
    - **Ch 15: It's Supposed to Be Hard:** Paying the price of admission, the Donner Party's deadly shortcut, and Lawrence of Arabia's match trick.
    - **Ch 16: Keep Running:** The decay of competitive advantages, the Red Queen Effect, and the rise and fall of Sears.
    - **Ch 17: The Wonders of the Future:** Why we chronically underestimate compounding progress, Thomas Edison's 1908 forecast, and Polaroid's accidental discovery.
    - **Ch 18: Harder Than It Looks and Not as Fun as It Seems:** Curated highlight reels, hidden backstage misery, and why the grass is greener where it is fertilised with nonsense.
4. **[Part 4: Enduring Truths](part-4-enduring-truths.md)** (Ch 19–23)
    - **Ch 19: Incentives: The Most Powerful Force in the World:** How crazy incentives justify crazy behaviour, Upton Sinclair's rule, and questioning your own blind spots.
    - **Ch 20: Now You Get It:** Intellectual knowledge vs. visceral lived experience, WWII soldiers under fire, and why "buy the dip" hurts in real life.
    - **Ch 21: Time Horizons:** Why the long run is just a string of painful short runs, and why flexibility beats rigid multi-year forecasts.
    - **Ch 22: Trying Too Hard:** Complexity as an illusion of control, no extra points for difficulty, and the power of simple, boring fundamentals.
    - **Ch 23: Wounds Heal, Scars Last:** How personal trauma shapes permanent worldviews, generational risk tolerance, and why disagreements are often about lived history.
