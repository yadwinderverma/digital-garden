# Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind

**Author:** Yuval Noah Harari  
**Status:** Listening

## Core Thesis

*Homo sapiens* came to dominate the planet because it is the only animal capable of cooperating flexibly in very large numbers. That large-scale cooperation rests on our unique ability to believe in shared fictions: intersubjective realities such as money, nations, corporations, human rights, and laws.

## Parts & Chapter Notes

1. **[Part 1: The Cognitive Revolution](part-1-cognitive-revolution.md)** (c. 70,000 BCE)
    - **Ch 1: An Animal of No Significance:** Sibling human species, jumbo brains, fire/cooking, and the hasty jump to the top of the food chain.
    - **Ch 2: The Tree of Knowledge:** Gossip, fictive language, Dunbar's number (~150), the Legend of Peugeot, and cultural evolution bypassing DNA.
    - **Ch 3: A Day in the Life of Adam and Eve:** Stone Age brains ("gorging gene"), the original affluent society, animism, Sungir burials, and the Aché people.
    - **Ch 4: The Flood:** Stone Age ecological serial killers; megafauna extinctions in Australia, the Americas, and Wrangel Island.
2. **[Part 2: The Agricultural Revolution](part-2-agricultural-revolution.md)** (c. 10,000 BCE)
    - **Ch 5: History's Biggest Fraud:** How wheat domesticated *Sapiens*, DNA copies vs. individual suffering, the Luxury Trap, Göbekli Tepe, and animal domestication.
    - **Ch 6: Building Pyramids:** Imagined orders, Hammurabi's Code vs. the US Declaration of Independence, and why you want a holiday in Paris.
    - **Ch 7: Memory Overload:** Bureaucracy, the first recorded name in history (Kushim the accountant), Sumerian cuneiform, and Inca quipus.
    - **Ch 8: There is No Justice in History:** Arbitrary hierarchies, purity myths, "biology enables, culture forbids", American slavery's vicious circle, and Louis XIV's wig.
3. **[Part 3: The Unification of Humankind](part-3-unification-of-humankind.md)**
    - **Ch 9: The Arrow of History:** Cognitive dissonance as culture's engine, global convergence, and the illusion of "authentic" national dishes.
    - **Ch 10: The Scent of Money:** Universal trust, Cortés's "disease of the heart", Osama bin Laden and US dollars, and Christian kings minting Arabic coins.
    - **Ch 11: Imperial Visions:** Cultural blending, the Numantia paradox, and India's Mughal and British inheritances.
    - **Ch 12: The Law of Religion:** Polytheistic tolerance vs. St Bartholomew's Day, Buddhism and craving, and modern humanist "religions".
    - **Ch 13: The Secret of Success:** Level-two chaos, Constantine's coin flip, Marx's self-defeating prediction, and why we study history.
4. **[Part 4: The Scientific Revolution](part-4-scientific-revolution.md)** (c. 1500 CE – present)
    - **Ch 14: The Discovery of Ignorance:** Admitting *ignoramus*, the Gilgamesh Project, and two Scottish ministers who invented actuarial finance.
    - **Ch 15: The Marriage of Science and Empire:** Empty maps, Vespucci vs. Columbus, Captain Cook's *Endeavour*, and Admiral Zheng He.
    - **Ch 16: The Capitalist Creed:** Credit as trust in tomorrow, the Mississippi Bubble, private corporations conquering India/Indonesia, and the Opium Wars.
    - **Ch 17: The Wheels of Industry:** Energy conversion, railway timetables inventing uniform time, factory farming, Harry Harlow's monkeys, and consumerism.
    - **Ch 18: A Permanent Revolution:** State & market replacing family, why war stopped paying (Silicon Valley), and sugar killing more than gunpowder.
    - **Ch 19: And They Lived Happily Ever After:** Expectations, biochemical thermostats, narrative meaning, and the lottery winner vs. the medieval peasant.
    - **Ch 20: The End of *Homo Sapiens*:** Bioengineering, cyborgs, inorganic life, Alba the glowing green rabbit, and *"What do we want to want?"*
