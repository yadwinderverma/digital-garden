# Part 1: The Cognitive Revolution

## Key Takeaways

- **Multiple Human Species:** For most of history, *Homo sapiens* shared the planet with several other human species (*Homo neanderthalensis*, *Homo erectus*, *Homo soloensis*, *Homo denisova*). Our exclusive survival was not inevitable.
- **The Cost of Large Brains:** Giant brains consume roughly 25% of resting energy and required premature birth, creating intense social dependencies for child-rearing.
- **Three Levels of Language:**
    1. **Objective warnings:** "Careful, a lion!" (shared with green monkeys and birds).
    2. **Social gossip:** Tracking who is trustworthy, who cheats, and who aligns with whom in bands of up to ~150 individuals (**Dunbar's number**).
    3. **Fictive language (The Tree of Knowledge mutation):** Speaking about things that do not physically exist at all (tribal spirits, nations, limited liability companies).

!!! note "Why Peugeot Exists"
    Harari uses the automaker Peugeot as a prime example of an imagined reality. Peugeot is not its factories, vehicles, or employees; if all of them vanished, the legal entity could still borrow money and hire new staff. It exists purely because collective legal agreements say it does.

## Questions & Reflections from AI Chats

- **How do organizations scale past 150 people without collapsing into bureaucracy?** Small teams rely on peer trust (gossip); beyond ~150 people, alignment requires explicit shared narratives, protocols, and institutional rules.
- **Rapid Cultural Evolution:** Unlike genetic evolution, which takes tens of thousands of years to alter behaviour, changing a shared myth (e.g. from divine right of kings to popular sovereignty) rewires collective human behaviour in a single generation.
