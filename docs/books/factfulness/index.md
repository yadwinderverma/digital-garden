# Factfulness: Ten Reasons We're Wrong About the World - and Why Things Are Better Than You Think

**Authors:** Hans Rosling, Ola Rosling, and Anna Rosling Rönnlund  
**Status:** Listened

## Core Thesis

People across every profession and education level (including Nobel laureates, journalists, and CEOs) systematically get basic facts about the world wrong, often scoring worse than random-guessing chimpanzees. The culprit is not a lack of intelligence or access to data, but an **overdramatic worldview** powered by ten deep-seated cognitive instincts that evolved for survival on the savannah rather than for interpreting global statistics. *Factfulness* is the stress-reducing habit of only carrying opinions for which you have strong supporting facts, replacing the outdated "developed vs. developing" binary with a fact-based, four-level income framework.

## The Four Income Levels

Instead of splitting the world into two outdated boxes ("rich" and "poor"), Rosling divides humanity into four income levels (measured in daily income per person, adjusted for price differences):

- **Level 1 (Less than $2/day, ~1 billion people):** Extreme poverty. Walking barefoot, fetching water in buckets from a distant source, cooking over an open fire, sleeping on a dirt floor, and eating the same grey porridge every day.
- **Level 2 ($2 to $8/day, ~3 billion people):** Buying sandals, a bicycle, and a gas burner. Children go to school instead of spending hours fetching water; families sleep on a mattress and can afford chickens or eggs.
- **Level 3 ($8 to $32/day, ~2 billion people):** Cold tap water indoors, stable electricity, a fridge, a motorbike, and secondary education for children. Families can save for a rainy day and take an occasional holiday.
- **Level 4 (More than $32/day, ~1 billion people):** Hot and cold running water, a car, more than 12 years of education, and eating out or flying on holiday. Most readers live here, and from this height Levels 1, 2, and 3 all blur together as "poor".
